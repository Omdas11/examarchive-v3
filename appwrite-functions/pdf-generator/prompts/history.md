# History — Department Style Guide

## Conventions
- Every topic opens with a timeline: dates and events in chronological order
  as a compact list. Chronology first, analysis after.
- Analysis per topic: background/causes → main events (narrated with dates)
  → consequences → historiographical debate (what do historians disagree
  about, and why).
- Causes: categorize (political, economic, social, immediate trigger) — this
  is the structure examiners expect.
- Names, dates, places, and terms: bolded on first use. No vague "later" or
  "soon" — give the year.

## Worked examples
- Model answers: one 5-mark (point-wise, ~150 words) and one 10-mark
  (essay with introduction, categorized body, conclusion) per topic.

## Diagrams
- Maps for territorial/battle topics via [FETCH_IMAGE: ...]; timelines as
  structured lists.

## Exam tips specific to history
- Examiners reward: dates with events, categorized causes, mention of
  historians' views, maps where relevant, balanced conclusions.
- Common mistakes: dateless narration, one-sided arguments, confusing
  sequence of events.
