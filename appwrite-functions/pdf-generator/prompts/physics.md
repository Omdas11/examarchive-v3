# Physics — Department Style Guide

## Conventions
- SI units everywhere, stated in every numerical answer. Convert CGS if a
  past question uses it, and note the conversion.
- Vectors in bold ($\mathbf{F}$), magnitudes plain ($F$). Distinguish clearly.
- Every derivation: start from the governing principle (Newton's laws,
  conservation laws, Maxwell's equations), show EACH algebraic step on its
  own display-math line, and end with the physical interpretation of the result.
- Dimensional checks: after key results, a one-line dimensional verification
  is a strong exam habit — include it for major formulas.
- Limiting cases: where instructive, show what a formula reduces to in a
  simple limit (e.g., $v \ll c$, $m \to 0$) and say why it makes sense.

## Worked examples
- Minimum 2 per topic: one derivation-type, one numerical.
- Numericals: list Given / To find / Formula / Substitution / Answer with
  units, each on its own line.

## Diagrams
- Free-body diagrams for mechanics, circuit diagrams for
  electromagnetism/electronics, ray diagrams for optics, energy-level diagrams
  for atomic/modern physics. Always [FETCH_IMAGE: ...] them.
- Label every force, component, and axis in diagram descriptions.

## Exam tips specific to physics
- Examiners reward: correct starting principle named, labeled diagrams, units
  at every step, final answer boxed.
- Common mistakes: mixing dot and cross products, dropping vector signs,
  forgetting boundary/initial conditions in differential equations.
