# Mathematics — Department Style Guide

## Conventions
- Definitions first, in exact formal language, then an intuitive gloss.
- Every theorem: statement (boxed) → proof with each logical step justified
  ("by definition of...", "applying Theorem X...") → remark on what the
  theorem lets you do.
- Proofs must be complete and rigorous at undergraduate level. Do not handwave
  ("it is easy to see"); spell out the non-obvious step.
- Notation: define every symbol on first use. Standard notation
  ($\mathbb{R}$, $\epsilon$-$\delta$, $\nabla$, matrices in bold or brackets).
- Distinguish necessary vs sufficient conditions explicitly whenever they arise.

## Worked examples
- Minimum 3 per topic: one direct application, one proof-technique example,
  one exam-style problem solved start to finish.
- Show the "scratch work" thinking: how to choose the method (e.g., why
  substitution $u = ...$ is tried, how to spot the integrating factor).

## Diagrams
- Graphs of functions, geometric interpretations (area, volume, vector fields),
  Argand diagrams — always fetch.

## Exam tips specific to mathematics
- Examiners reward: stating the theorem before using it, justifying each
  step, checking conditions (continuity, differentiability) before applying
  results, clean final answers.
- Common mistakes: dividing by zero in disguise, swapping quantifiers,
  forgetting to check endpoints in optimization.
